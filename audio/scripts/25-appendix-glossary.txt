Appendix. Glossary.

Each entry gives a one-line reminder of a term, and the chapter that teaches the term has the full account.

An accumulation proof is the prover's message that lets a verifier check one accumulation step.

An accumulator is a running object that carries deferred checks from one recursive step to the next.

An action set indicator is a value used to find out which actions a stamp covers.

An anchor is a hash-chain value that commits to the Tachyon pool state.

An anomalous curve is a curve whose number of points equals its field size, which cycles have to avoid.

Atomic accumulation is accumulation in which the accumulator alone is enough to verify each step.

Autonome, aggregate and adjunct are the three states of a Tachyon bundle. An autonome has a complete proof, an aggregate has a merged proof, and an adjunct is a stripped input to a merged proof.

The base case is the start of a recursive chain, where no earlier proof exists.

A batched opening is a reduction of many polynomial evaluation claims to one.

Binding is the property that a prover cannot open one commitment to two different values.

The binding signature is the signature that enforces a bundle's value balance through homomorphic value commitments.

A bonding polynomial is the wiring polynomial of a circuit with constraints only, and it equals zero whenever its second variable is zero.

A bucket is a group of an epoch's tachygrams that share a quadratic-residue profile.

Compression means replacing a large proof's polynomials with commitments and openings.

The consensus window is the two epochs that validators check for duplicate tachygrams.

A cross term, in the inner product argument, is one of the two off-diagonal points, named L and R, sent in each halving round.

A curve cycle, also called a two-cycle, is two curves in which each curve's order equals the other's field size.

A decider is the party or step that runs a deferred check.

A deferred operation is work that one circuit hands to the circuit on the other curve.

An endomorphism is a map from a curve to itself that respects addition. On curves with j equal to zero, it acts as multiplication by an eigenvalue.

An endoscalar is a short challenge that the curve's endomorphism makes cheap to apply.

An epoch nullifier is the value a note publishes for a given epoch.

The error matrix is the matrix of all revdot products between claim vectors, and its diagonal holds the claimed values.

An evidence tree is a tree that retains one proof for all of an epoch's buckets.

An exclusion proof is a proof that a nullifier is absent from closed epochs.

Faerie gold is an attack in which a sender makes two notes that cannot both be spent.

The Fiat-Shamir transform replaces a verifier's random challenges with a hash of the transcript.

Folding means halving a claim with a random challenge.

A folding scheme is a reduction of several instances of a relation to one, with no verifier run inside.

Fuse is Ragu's operation that produces a node's proof from its children.

A gate is one multiplication check on the witness, requiring the gate's a wire times its b wire to equal its c wire.

Grinding means retrying offline until a hash-derived challenge favors the prover.

A header is a node's public claim in proof-carrying data.

Hiding is the property that a commitment reveals nothing about the committed value.

Hoisting means sharing products between gadget steps to save gates.

Incrementally verifiable computation, or IVC, is a proof that covers a whole chain of steps.

An indexed factor is the cubic encoding of a pair of epoch and nullifier, used to build multisets.

An inner product argument, or IPA, is a short proof of a claim about the inner product of committed vectors.

An instance is the public part of a statement.

A master key is a per-note key derived with Poseidon, from which the note's nullifiers come.

The max fee note and the fee change note are the values that let a bundle's fee be chosen after signing.

A multi-scalar multiplication, or MSM, is a sum of many scalar multiples of points, which dominates IPA verification.

A NARK is a non-interactive proof with a constant-size instance and a linear-size witness.

A nested commitment is a commitment to the coordinates of points from the other curve.

Non-native arithmetic means emulating one field's arithmetic inside a circuit over another field.

A nullifier is a value published on spend to stop double spends.

A payment key is a hash of the spend validating key and the nullifier key that a note commits to.

A Pedersen vector commitment is the point you get by multiplying each entry of a vector by the generator for its position and adding the results, optionally with a blinding term.

A polynomial commitment scheme, or PCS, is a commitment to a polynomial, together with a way to prove its evaluations.

A polynomial IOP is a protocol in which the verifier may query polynomial oracles.

Post-processing is Halo's technique for supporting many circuits without verification keys.

Proof-carrying data, or PCD, is a computation structured as a tree of steps, where each step's data carries a proof of all the work behind it.

A quadratic arithmetic program, or QAP, is a polynomial form of R one CS in which the constraints hold when one polynomial divides another.

The quotient fold is Ragu's fuse-time reduction of revdot claims using Lagrange weights and a quotient.

A quotient polynomial comes from subtracting a claimed value from a polynomial and dividing by X minus the evaluation point. It exists exactly when the polynomial takes the claimed value at that point.

A rank-one constraint system, or R one CS, has constraints saying that matrix A times the witness, multiplied entry by entry with matrix B times the witness, equals matrix C times the witness.

A random linear combination merges several claims with random weights.

A random oracle is an idealized hash function whose outputs behave as independent random values.

A registry polynomial is a polynomial that interpolates many circuits' wiring over a domain.

A revdot claim is an instance made of two commitments and a value, with witness vectors whose revdot product is that value.

A revdot product is the inner product of one vector with the reversal of another.

The Schwartz-Zippel lemma says that a nonzero low-degree polynomial is zero at a random point with probability at most its degree divided by the size of the field.

Soundness is the property that false statements rarely pass verification.

A spendable is a proof that certifies a note's position in the pool's history.

Split accumulation is accumulation in which the verifier handles only public instances, while the prover keeps a separate witness.

A stage mask is a gate-free revdot claim that a stage polynomial's forbidden wires are zero.

A stage polynomial is a separately committed part of a circuit's trace.

A stamp is the proof-bearing part of a Tachyon bundle, containing tachygrams, an anchor, and a proof.

A step is the rule relating a node's input headers to its output header.

A structured vector is a vector arranged so that the revdot product pairs each gate's a and b wires.

Succinct verification means in-circuit verifier cost that grows more slowly than the circuit.

A sync service is an untrusted party that proves nullifier absence without learning which note it serves.

A tachygram is an opaque field element published for each action, either a note commitment or a nullifier.

Transcript bridging means using challenges from one curve's transcript in the circuits on both curves.

Trial decryption is a wallet's attempt to decrypt every output to find its own.

The unified instance is the shared public values of Ragu's internal circuits.

A vanishing polynomial is the polynomial that is zero on every point of a domain.

A verification key is circuit-specific data that many systems require and Ragu avoids.

The witness is the secret values that satisfy a circuit, also called the trace.

That is the last entry, and it completes the glossary.
