Chapter twenty-five. Final Recap.

This chapter follows one spend from a wallet's note to a block, and it names the chapter that explains each part, so that you can return to whichever one you want to hear again. It adds no protocol content. The derivations, the numbers, and the arguments about cheating all stay in the chapters we name.

Tachyon has not activated on mainnet, and its design is still changing. The trace reads Tachyon and Ragu at the commits this site pins, while Tachyon itself pins a different commit of Ragu. We found no real Tachyon circuits and no validator at these commits. So when a sentence here says that a step checks something, it describes what the design asks for, and a section near the end separates what exists from what does not.

The spend we follow.

Alice holds a note from Bob, created in epoch three and spent with target epoch seven. Her bundle has one spend and one output to Carol, and the chapter on the proof tree carries it as far as a published stamp. Most of the values in those chapters come from stand-in hashes rather than real Poseidon outputs. The chapter on aggregation and bundles uses bundles of its own, and its bundle Y also has one spend and one output, so Y can stand in for Alice's bundle there.

Most of this chapter follows the spend through Tachyon. One section pauses at a single node of the proof tree to go through the proof system underneath it, and a later section lists every layer with its chapter.

The note reaches Alice.

Alice's wallet derives two children from its spending key, one for authorizing spends and one for deriving nullifiers. Bob needs a way to name her as the owner without learning her nullifier key, so she gives him a payment key, which is a hash of her spend validating key and her nullifier key.

Bob then forms the note from the payment key, the value, and two trapdoors, and he computes its commitment. Alice has to learn all four fields, and Tachyon leaves that to a payment protocol outside the shielded protocol, which frees her wallet from trial-decrypting all traffic. Her wallet should also compare the note against her others, because a sender who reuses the nullifier trapdoor can make one of her notes unspendable.

The pool records the commitment.

Bob's stamp publishes the note commitment as a tachygram, the single kind of value that covers both commitments and nullifiers. Validators will eventually forget that tachygram, yet Alice has to prove facts about it later, so the pool keeps a short reference to it.

The stamp's tachygrams become the roots of a polynomial, and a Pedersen commitment shrinks that polynomial to one point. A Poseidon hash chain absorbs the point together with the block's epoch, and a boundary digest joins each epoch to the next. The chain's state is the anchor, and the chapter on the proof tree lists the anchors this note lives through. A proof can witness history freely, because one consensus check at the far end pins every earlier state.

What the spend has to show.

Alice's nullifiers come from a master key and a sponge that gives a different value for each epoch. The note's commitment pins them, so she has no choice about their values. Validators hold only two epochs of tachygrams, and this divides her double-spend obligations in two.

For each of the closed epochs three to six, she proves that the epoch's nullifier is absent from that epoch, since validators no longer hold all of that history. For epochs seven and eight, she publishes the two nullifiers. The spend might land in its target epoch or the next. Publishing the pair means that a spend accepted in either epoch puts that epoch's nullifier there, where a later test will find it. The proof makes no claim about epoch seven itself, because the window covers it.

The sync service tests old nullifiers.

Testing absence means reading whole epochs, which is the work wallets were supposed to stop doing. Alice can hand it to a service because each epoch's value is unrelated to the others. She sends explicit values and no key, and she has to keep the nullifiers for epochs seven and eight out of every request.

An epoch's polynomial is too large to open directly, so a builder routes its tachygrams into buckets by quadratic-residue profile. Each bucket's seal computes the crossing, so that it covers the whole epoch. The service opens the matching bucket at the value and composes the results into a segment, an Arbitrary Unspent proof that names no note. The proof tree chapter gives the service all four epochs and uses one bucket per epoch to keep the numbers short, while the oblivious sync chapter's run delegates a narrower range. Both are choices of example, and a wallet may run these steps itself.

The wallet builds the proof tree.

Alice's wallet now combines its secrets with the service's segments, working up the tree one step at a time. First, Note Seed pairs the note commitment with the master key, and Nullifier Derive commits a window of epoch and nullifier pairs as a product of irreducible cubic factors. Second, Unspent Bind divides the service's polynomial out of that window, using a factor that carries its epoch. This is the first point at which the service's work belongs to a note.

Third, QR Spendable Init consumes the test of epoch three and finds the note commitment in that epoch's bucket, and Spendable Lift moves the lineage to the entry anchor of epoch seven. Fourth, Spend Bind derives the nullifiers for epochs seven and eight and the value commitment, and Spend Action derives the randomized key and builds the action and tachygram sets.

Fifth, Output Bind and Output Action create Carol's note, with the same header type and two tachygrams. Sixth, Action Lift and Action Merge bring the two actions to one anchor and combine them. Seventh, Fee Fuse applies a fee chosen after signing, and it turns an actions header into a stamp exactly once. Eighth, Stamp Lift moves the stamp to a later anchor in epoch seven.

The whole tree appears in chapter twenty-three, which flags its exact mix of steps as its own reconstruction. Two facts remain outside the proof. A signature under the randomized key requires the spend authorizing key, which lets a custody device hold the key while another device proves. And since no step adds up values, a binding signature shows that the bundle balances.

What one fuse proves.

Every node in that tree is one call to fuse, which runs a step on two child proofs and emits a header and a proof. Take Spend Bind as the node, and follow what its proof has to contain.

Its step first becomes gates and linear constraints, and random powers reduce those to one consolidated equation. Ragu reads that equation as one coefficient of a polynomial product, where a QAP would use a quotient by a vanishing polynomial. The prover commits to its polynomials, and many evaluation claims batch into one, which an inner product argument can open. Random checks and Fiat-Shamir remove the interaction, and chapter nine assembles these parts into one complete proof.

That proof leaves the verifier two linear costs, a wiring value that the site writes as s of x and y, and a combined generator that the site writes as G hat. The node has to verify its children inside its own circuit, and those costs block this. So the rest of the proof system moves that work out of the circuit, along with the checks that read whole vectors.

Accumulation turns G hat into a claim that travels with the proof and merges with others, and splitting the accumulator keeps its witness out of the circuit, so a fuse runs no inner product argument. Revdot folding carries the product claims forward in the same way. The registry replaces s of x and y with claims about one polynomial holding every circuit, so a node pays only for the step it chooses. The Pasta cycle lets each side handle the other's points natively, endoscalars carry challenges across it, and stages let circuits share data.

Chapter eighteen, on recursion in Ragu, assembles these into many small internal circuits. Three accumulators per side pass up the tree, and each fuse replaces two of each with one, so Spend Bind's proof has the same shape as the proof at the top. A proof that leaves the tree goes through compression, which replaces its polynomials with commitments and a few values and finishes with an inner product argument on each curve. Its verifier pays the deferred linear costs once, and chapter nineteen, on compression, gives the size of the result.

From the stamp to a block.

Alice's wallet publishes her bundle as an autonome. Her signatures cover only effecting data, so the stamp in the trailer can change without invalidating them. A miner could include the bundle as it stands, and a validator would rebuild its header from published data.

An aggregator may instead merge it with other stamps. It lifts its inputs to one anchor and keeps them disjoint, and it needs none of Alice's secrets, because its steps read only commitments and published values. If a block mines one of the inputs first, Stamp Unmerge divides it out.

A miner then strips each covered bundle, so Alice's bundle keeps its body and fee and gains a pointer to the aggregate's W T X I D. The T X I D half of the identifier stays stable through all of this, so her wallet can still recognize the bundle.

The block's validators then check signatures, pointers, coverage, the tachygram count, the set commitment, and the proof. They also run the consensus rules, which look up the anchor, require its epoch to be current or preceding, check each tachygram against the window, and absorb the commitment into the chain. Finding the anchor closes the free witnesses below it. Alice's nullifiers are now in the pool, and a second spend of this note runs into either the window or the exclusion proof.

This sequence is reconstructed from the code at the commit this site pins, and the authors have not confirmed it. The crate has no block validator, so the sequence combines the bundle verify method with consensus rules from the book. Chapter twenty-four, on aggregation, and chapter twenty-one, on pool state, both note that this is a reading of the sources, and that no source fixes the full order.

What exists at the pinned commits.

The trace sounds like one working system, but its parts have very different status, and each chapter's warnings give the details.

First, here is what we read as code. Ragu at the commit this site pins contains fuse, compress, and their verifiers, although we did not build or run it or run its benchmarks. The Tachyon crate contains the proof steps, written as native step bodies, along with the fold functions, the wire format, and a bundle verify method for one covering bundle.

Second, here is what the sources describe and we did not find in code. There are no real Tachyon circuits, because the crate builds against a mock at the Ragu commit that Tachyon pins. That mock's fuse runs a step natively, discards the polynomial openings, and does not verify child proofs. There is no validator and no mempool. The window and the anchor lookup appear only in the book and in doc comments, and block assembly and transaction-level balance appear only in the book and in tests. There is no normative specification, because the shielded protocol, accumulator, syncing service, and bundle and aggregator ZIPs are empty outlines. And the crate has no wallet check for reused trapdoors.

Third, here is what remains open in the design. Zero knowledge and unlinkability are not yet established, although the Tachyon book assumes them. Decompression has not landed, so a compressed proof cannot be fused today, and an aggregator's real costs are unknown. The sources differ on which anchors consensus accepts and where the crossing sits. Service privacy measures, fee levels, aggregator payment, and per-note proving delegation are unspecified or unsettled. The comparison with Orchard is an estimate, and real proof size is not settled for Tachyon.

Where this chapter leaves us.

A note enters the pool as a tachygram, and the pool refers to it through a polynomial commitment absorbed into a hash chain. A spend proves absence for closed epochs, with help from a service that does not know the note, and it publishes a nullifier pair for the window to check. Each step of the wallet's tree is one fuse, and Ragu's accumulators keep the proof the same shape at every depth. A stamp can pass through an aggregator and a miner without disturbing what Alice signed, and validators check the result against the anchor chain. All of this is a design at fixed commits, with mock proofs on the Tachyon side and no validator, and any later commit may differ.
